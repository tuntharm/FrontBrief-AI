## Web Edition

Today's frontier is defined by a single tension: the biggest claims in AI are now arriving pre-loaded with disputes about whether they're real — or legal. A US-China fight over model distillation, a contested "perfect score" at the world's toughest math contest, a record copyright bill, and a founder comeback that repriced robotics all landed in the same 48 hours. The through-line is that provenance, reproducibility, and legal exposure have become first-order questions, not afterthoughts.

### Washington turns a distillation dispute into a sanctions threat
The White House has accused Chinese lab Moonshot AI of covertly distilling Anthropic's frontier "Fable" model to build its Kimi K3 open-weights release, and Treasury Secretary Bessent has [threatened Entity List sanctions](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/22/treasury-threatens-sanctions-after-white-house-claims-moonshot-distilled-anthropics-fable/), posting the viral line "Open source is not open season on American IP." The technical case is shaky: Fable has only been public since around July 1, and [experts dispute how a roughly 15-day window could explain Kimi K3's quality](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/23/experts-say-exploiting-anthropics-fable-isnt-how-kimi-k3-got-so-good/), arguing distillation alone can't account for it. Whatever the forensics show, the episode is precedent-setting — it turns open-weights strategy and distillation risk into a matter of export controls and national security, and puts every frontier lab's IP posture on notice.

### A "perfect" Olympiad score meets a credibility test
Two Chinese systems — Huawei's "Celia" and RedNote/Xiaohongshu's "dots-note-3.0" — claim a flawless 42/42 across all six problems at the 2026 International Math Olympiad in Shanghai, a score only 7 of 666 human contestants matched. But the result is now the subject of a public fight: IMO president Gregor Dolinar and Fields Medalist Terence Tao have [questioned the compute used, the degree of human involvement, and the reproducibility](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/mathematicians-question-ai-performance-at-international-math-olympiad/) of the runs, including concerns about "best-of-n self-grading." As [regional coverage of the dispute](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/world/archives/2026/07/24/2003861308) notes, the results can't currently be officially validated — a reminder that a landmark reasoning claim is only as strong as the protocol behind it.

### Google trims its workhorse models and names Gemini 4
Google refreshed its mid-tier lineup with [Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and a 3.5 Flash Cyber security variant](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-6-flash-3-5-flash-lite-3-5-flash-cyber/), cutting output tokens by roughly 17% alongside price reductions — and, for the first time, naming Gemini 4 as an active project in "the most ambitious pre-training run yet." The [conspicuous absence of a 3.5 Pro flagship](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/google-releases-three-new-gemini-models-but-no-3-5-pro/) drew notice, while the Flash Cyber variant flagged 55 confirmed bugs in Google's own V8 engine — against 36 for Claude Opus and 47 for standard Flash — signalling that security-specialized models are becoming their own product category as the workhorse tier keeps getting cheaper.

### Travis Kalanick is back, and he brought $1.7 billion
Uber's ousted founder has returned with one of 2026's largest private rounds: his robotics venture Atoms [raised $1.7 billion led by a16z](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/22/travis-kalanicks-robotics-company-raises-1-7b-led-by-a16z/), with Ben Horowitz declaring "Travis is back" and Uber itself investing in the man it once forced out. The company is pitching "physical AI" across food, mining, and transport — and the sheer size of the check, plus the marquee backers, makes it a defining statement on the embodied-AI thesis and a signal of where late-stage capital is now rotating.

### The price of pirated training data is now on the record
A federal judge has given final approval to Anthropic's [$1.5 billion settlement](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-07-20/us-judge-approves-anthropics-1-5-billion-settlement-of-copyright-lawsuit) with authors over pirated books — roughly $3,000 per book across about 500,000 works, the largest copyright payout in US history. Beyond the headline number, it establishes a concrete reference price for AI training-data liability, giving every lab and investor a figure to plug into their risk models and raising the diligence bar on where training corpora come from.

The money is telling a clear story: the largest checks are rotating toward embodied AI, with Kalanick's $1.7B round and a fresh European humanoid unicorn marking robotics as this cycle's funding magnet, while Google's price cuts keep squeezing inference margins and Samsung's reported ~€1B talks with Mistral show sovereign-adjacent bets continuing. What to watch next: whether Treasury actually moves Moonshot toward the Entity List and how the Fable distillation forensics resolve; whether the IMO's disputed 42/42 is backed by reproducible, compute-disclosed protocols or quietly fades; and the next embodied-AI mega-round after Atoms. The common thread — provenance and proof — is becoming the frontier's real currency.
