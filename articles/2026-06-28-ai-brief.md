## Web Edition

This week the frontier was about contestability — who can defend their model's IP, who controls the consumer market, and how quickly a model generation can be displaced. Anthropic took an alleged model-theft campaign to the US Senate, fresh venture money rotated toward robots that learn from video games and drugs designed by AI, and the once-unassailable ChatGPT quietly lost its last GPT-4-era model and, by some measures, its majority of the assistant market. The through-line: nothing at the frontier is settled — not the IP, not the market share, not the reigning model.

### Anthropic takes a distillation fight to the Senate
Anthropic told US senators that Alibaba's Qwen lab ran what it called the largest known distillation attack on its models, [using roughly 25,000 fake accounts and 28.8 million exchanges](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/anthropic-alibaba-distillation-campaign.html) between late April and early June to extract Claude's capabilities. Senators are now drafting an amendment to sanction or blacklist those responsible. Beyond the immediate accusation, [the episode reopens a harder question](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-accuses-alibaba-distillation-claude-qwen): is distillation even theft? However that line is drawn, model access — not just chips — looks set to become an export-control battleground between the US and China.

### Robots that learn from video games
General Intuition raised a $320M Series A at a $2.3B valuation [on the bet that gameplay is the best training data for the physical world](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/general-intuitions-2-3b-bet-that-video-games-can-train-ai-agents-for-the-real-world/). The company is building spatial, embodied frontier models trained on billions of game clips drawn from some 17 million players via the Medal platform, arguing that button-press-timed gameplay teaches agents how to act in space better than scarce teleoperated robot data. Khosla Ventures led the round, [with Jeff Bezos and Eric Schmidt among the backers](https://gamesbeat.com/general-intuition-raises-320m-at-2-3b-valuation-for-ai-frontier-models-based-on-gameplay-exclusive-interview/) — a cap table that signals how seriously the embodied-AI data thesis is now being taken.

### AI drug discovery starts to show its receipts
At the BIO 2026 convention, AI-discovered drugs moved from promise toward proof, with the anchor signal a collaboration [worth up to $2.5B between Insilico Medicine and SK Biopharmaceuticals](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/insilico-medicine-and-sk-biopharmaceuticals-achieved-ai-powered-drug-discovery-collaboration-worth-up-to-2-5-billion-for-neuroimmune-disorders-302806072.html) on neuroimmune and central-nervous-system disorders. The deal is concrete evidence that AI drug discovery is converting into pipeline value rather than demos — and it runs against a tense geopolitical backdrop, with the BIOSECURE Act shadowing how US and Chinese biotech firms partner.

### The quiet end of the GPT-4 era
With little fanfare, OpenAI [removed GPT-4.5 from ChatGPT](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/openai-just-quietly-retired-the-last-of-the-gpt-4-models-and-it-feels-like-the-end-of-an-ai-era), the last model of the GPT-4 family in the product, with conversations now falling back to GPT-5.5. The model line that defined the modern chatbot era is gone from the flagship product (API access remains a separate track), and the retirement is as much symbolic as practical: it marks how sharply the frontier now compresses a model's shelf life, and how the cadence of retiring and re-anchoring flagships has itself become a competitive variable.

### ChatGPT's grip on the assistant market slips
For the first time, ChatGPT has fallen below half of global AI-assistant usage. According to [widely-cited usage trackers](https://unrot.co/blogs/today-top-10-ai-news-june-26-2026), its share slipped to roughly 46.4% as Gemini (around 27.7%) and Claude (around 10.3%) gained ground — figures drawn from usage-tracker roundups rather than an official OpenAI release, so best read as directional. The milestone, if it holds, reframes the consumer AI layer as a genuine three-way contest in which distribution and defaults may matter as much as raw model quality.

The money this week followed two currents. The first is contestability of the consumer layer: a sub-50% ChatGPT and the retirement of the last GPT-4-era model together suggest the assistant market is now genuinely up for grabs, with distribution and defaults rising in importance. The second is rotation: fresh frontier dollars are landing less on bigger chatbots and more on embodied AI and AI biotech, where General Intuition's $2.3B valuation and Insilico's up-to-$2.5B deal mark the new surfaces. Watch the Senate's language on distillation, whether gameplay-derived data proves out for robotics, and whether Gemini and Claude can sustain their share gains.
