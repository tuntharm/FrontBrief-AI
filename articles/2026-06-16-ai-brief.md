## Top 5

### 1. Bezos returns as co-CEO of Project Prometheus, raising $12B at ~$41B
- Category: Must-Know — big-name founder / mega-funding
- Why it's interesting: Jeff Bezos has stepped back into an operating role as co-CEO (with ex-Google X scientist Vik Bajaj) of Project Prometheus, a "physical AI" lab building an "artificial general engineer" — AI to invent, design and manufacture physical products like engines and medicines. It raised ~$12B in June 2026 at roughly a $41B valuation (Bezos the largest individual backer; JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, DST Global and Arch Venture Partners also in), and has poached ~120 people from OpenAI, DeepMind, Meta, xAI and Nvidia.
- Why it matters: It's the clearest signal that the frontier is broadening from chatbots to "physical AI" / AI-for-engineering, and that the field's most powerful builders are personally betting on it with mega-capital and aggressive hiring.
- Investment angle: Physical AI is the new flagship category for capital and talent; watch which hardware, materials-science, manufacturing and robotics partners Prometheus signs, and the talent drain it inflicts on incumbent labs. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: A live template for deeptech-meets-foundation-models — the "AI designs and builds real things" thesis is exactly the founder/investor lane to study; note how they pair scientific talent with frontier-scale compute.
- Action: Read
- Link: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/jeff-bezoss-prometheus-raises-12b-to-build-an-artificial-general-engineer-for-the-physical-world/

### 2. China's Moonshot AI seeks up to $2B at a $30B valuation
- Category: Interesting Company / Startup
- Why it's interesting: Moonshot AI, the Beijing developer of the open Kimi models, is in talks to raise up to $2B at a ~$30B valuation — its third financing in roughly six months and about a sevenfold jump from ~$4B late last year. ARR crossed ~$200M in April as demand for low-cost, open-weight models surged.
- Why it matters: It shows how fast China's open-weight champions are compounding value and revenue, and that open models are becoming a credible pricing threat to closed US labs as enterprises trade down on cost.
- Investment angle: Open-weight model labs (especially China's) are a structural deflationary force on inference pricing; watch ARR growth and whether Western enterprises adopt Kimi-class open models to cut bills. (Signal, not advice; no live prices.)
- Tharm relevance: For anyone building on models, open-weight frontier challengers expand the cost/portability frontier — a reason to keep an abstraction layer and benchmark open models against closed APIs regularly.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://technode.com/2026/06/08/kimi-reaches-30-billion-valuation-after-sixfold-increase-in-half-a-year/

### 3. Crusoe raises $1.375B at a $10B+ valuation to build AI "factories"
- Category: Money / Market / NVIDIA / Compute
- Why it's interesting: AI-infrastructure builder Crusoe — which helped build OpenAI's first large US data center — closed an oversubscribed Series E of ~$1.375B at a valuation above $10B, co-led by Valor and Mubadala with Nvidia, Fidelity and Founders Fund participating. It says it has now contracted ~4.9 gigawatts of AI capacity and brought the first phase of its 1.2GW Abilene, Texas campus live within a year.
- Why it matters: It's another datapoint that the durable money is in capacity — power, land and gigawatt buildouts — and that the firms owning that capacity are repricing quickly while much of the app layer struggles to show willingness-to-pay.
- Investment angle: Picks-and-shovels (power, cooling, land, gigawatt campuses) remains the highest-conviction compute trade; watch contracted-capacity figures and energy access as the real moats. (Signal, not advice; no live prices.)
- Tharm relevance: When you model where surrogate/inference workloads will run, the constraint is increasingly power and contracted capacity, not chips alone — factor energy and siting into cost curves.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://www.crusoe.ai/resources/newsroom/crusoe-announces-series-e-funding

### 4. Microsoft's in-house MAI-Thinking-1 was preferred over Claude in blind tests
- Category: Product / Model / Adoption
- Why it's interesting: Microsoft's MAI-Thinking-1 — a ~35B-active-parameter mixture-of-experts reasoning model trained from scratch — matched Claude Opus 4.6 on a leading coding benchmark (SWE-Bench Pro) and was preferred over Claude Sonnet 4.6 by human raters in blind side-by-side tests, while posting strong math scores (e.g. ~97% AIME 2025, ~94.5% AIME 2026).
- Why it matters: It's the first credible third-party evidence that Microsoft can field a competitive frontier model in-house — a direct hedge against its dependence on OpenAI and Anthropic, and a sign hyperscalers will increasingly own their model stack.
- Investment angle: In-house hyperscaler models compress pricing power for pure model labs and reward whoever controls distribution; watch how Microsoft routes default workloads between MAI, OpenAI and Anthropic. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: A reminder that "best benchmark" rarely wins alone — distribution and cost control do. Design products so the underlying model is swappable as the competitive set churns.
- Action: Read
- Link: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317989/20260608/microsoft-built-its-own-ai-depend-openai-anthropic-less-says-it-just-beat-claude-blind-tests.htm

### 5. Nvidia ships Cosmos 3, an open "world foundation model" for physical AI
- Category: Deeptech / Research Lens
- Why it's interesting: Nvidia launched Cosmos 3, an open omnimodel for physical AI built on a mixture-of-transformers architecture and trained on ~20 trillion tokens of multimodal data (~1B images, ~400M real and synthetic videos, plus ambient sound and human/robot action). It targets world simulation, physical reasoning and action generation, with a Cosmos Coalition of robotics labs (Skild AI, Generalist, Black Forest Labs and others) building on it.
- Why it matters: World models are becoming the shared base layer for robotics, the way LLMs became the base layer for language — letting robots "understand the world before deploying in it" and dramatically cutting the cost of synthetic training data. It is also the open infrastructure powering the physical-AI gold rush that Prometheus epitomises.
- Investment angle: Defensibility shifts up the stack — to the policy models, evaluation, and "taste"/safety layers built on top of an open world model — rather than perception itself, which is commoditising. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: For scientific-ML and surrogate modelling, world/physics foundation models are a natural prior to fine-tune on — strong fit for the simulate-then-act loop; the premium is on rigorous validation and gated autonomy.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-launches-cosmos-3-the-open-frontier-foundation-model-for-physical-ai
