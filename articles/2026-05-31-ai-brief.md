- **Why it matters:** The most credible AGI forecaster at a tier-1 lab has publicly compressed the timeline by at least a year in one announcement. The accompanying framing — "little margin for error" — signals that internal confidence in near-term capability trajectories is high, not aspirational.
- **Founder/investor relevance:** Planning horizons for AI-native ventures need to compress. Positioning around post-AGI infrastructure — alignment tooling, oversight platforms, compute orchestration, and capability-gated deployment — is moving from speculative to near-term strategy. ### EU AI Act Digital Omnibus: 16-month compliance delay, new prohibited practices
- **Source:** Global Policy Watch
- **Link:** https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/
- **What happened:** On 7 May, EU institutions reached a provisional agreement on the Digital Omnibus on AI — the first amendment package to the AI Act since its June 2024 adoption. High-risk AI system (HRAIS) obligations are deferred from August 2026 to December 2027 (16 months). Synthetic content watermarking is deferred 4 months. Two new prohibited practices are added: AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM. The US side moved in parallel: NIST announced pre-deployment testing agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI.
- **Why it matters:** The deadline extension removes an immediate compliance pressure point for enterprises, but the underlying obligations are unchanged — the clock has just been reset. The new prohibitions signal that the EU is adding rather than removing scope. US pre-deployment gating formalises a checkpoint that could become a de-facto global standard.
- **Founder/investor relevance:** US companies building EU-facing AI products have an extra 16 months to instrument compliance, but should not treat this as a reprieve — use the time to build audit trails and documentation frameworks. The US pre-deployment agreements create a new category of "government-clearance-ready" AI infrastructure.
- **Action:** Monitor

### DeepSeek makes V4-Pro 75% price cut permanent as Huawei Ascend 950 scales
- **Source:** Technology.org / relvehq.com
- **Link:** https://www.technology.org/2026/05/25/deepseek-v4-pro-permanent-75-percent-price-cut/
- **What happened:** DeepSeek permanently locked in a 75% price reduction on its V4-Pro API on 25 May — a discount originally set to expire on 31 May. Pricing is now ~$0.0035–$0.83 per million tokens (down from $0.014–$3.33). V4-Pro runs on Huawei Ascend 950 chips, not Nvidia GPUs. Huawei plans to ship ~750,000 Ascend 950PR units this year, with full-scale shipments in H2 2026.
- **Why it matters:** This is a structural cost signal, not a promotional event. The Chinese inference stack is decoupling from Nvidia supply chains and approaching near-zero marginal cost. As Ascend 950 volume scales in H2, further price compression is likely. The pricing war between Western and Chinese frontier API providers is entering a permanent, not temporary, phase.
- **Founder/investor relevance:** Any product that uses API pricing as a competitive moat is exposed. Products built on DeepSeek or comparable Chinese models will have a structural cost advantage over products built on Anthropic or OpenAI APIs unless the Western labs respond. Monitor inference pricing monthly.

## Tharm's Deeptech Lens

### "Faster by Design": interactive aerodynamics via neural surrogates trained on expert-validated CFD
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18491v2
- **What happened:** A new arXiv paper demonstrates an aerodynamic surrogate model trained exclusively on expert-validated CFD data that enables sub-second aerodynamic evaluation across geometry perturbations during the design loop. The surrogate generalises within design bounds without retraining and matches expert-approved CFD within stated tolerances.
- **Why it matters:** The "expert-validated" training approach is the key contribution: rather than training on raw simulation ensembles, the surrogate learns from data that has already passed domain expert review, which is the credibility lever required for industrial adoption. This directly addresses the trust gap that has slowed surrogate model deployment in OEM design pipelines.
- **Founder/investor relevance:** Aerospace and automotive OEMs actively need this capability — sequential CFD calls are the primary bottleneck in aerodynamic design iterations. A surrogate fast enough for interactive use during design (not just post-hoc analysis) unlocks genuinely new workflows. This paper demonstrates productisable technology. Worth tracking the authors' institutional affiliation for potential collaboration or commercialisation activity.
- **Action:** Read now

## Founder / Investor Takeaway

The week's dominant signal is a convergence of timing pressure: Hassabis at 2029, NVIDIA at $91B guidance, and DeepSeek commoditising inference via a fully domestic chip stack. None of these are independent — they reinforce a single thesis that the AI compute and capability race is accelerating faster than most 2025 roadmaps assumed. For founders, the implication is that the design space for defensible AI products is narrowing: generic agentic platforms are consolidating around Sierra and OpenAI's TDC; inference cost is trending toward zero; and AGI framing is beginning to enter mainstream strategic planning. The ventures with durable positioning are those building proprietary data flywheels, domain-specific evaluation infrastructure, or post-AGI tooling. For investors, NVIDIA's 25x dividend raise and $18.6B direct-investment programme signal that the infrastructure super-cycle is being treated as a durable cash flow business, not a cyclical peak — that is the most significant market structure signal of the quarter.
