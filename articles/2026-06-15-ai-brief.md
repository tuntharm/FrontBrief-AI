## Top 5

### 1. US government forces Anthropic to suspend Claude Fable 5 & Mythos 5
- Category: Must-Know — frontier model / policy shock
- Why it's interesting: Anthropic launched Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (its most capable models) on June 9–10; on June 12 a US export-control directive ordered access blocked for any foreign national, anywhere. Unable to verify citizenship in real time, Anthropic disabled both models entirely — for everyone. Opus 4.8, Sonnet and Haiku still work; this is a regulatory recall, not an outage.
- Why it matters: Export controls have jumped from hardware to *model access*. A frontier model can now be switched off by government order, which makes single-vendor, single-jurisdiction dependence a live operational risk rather than a theoretical one.
- Investment angle: Tailwind for model-portability layers, evaluation/observability, and trusted-access/identity tooling; a fresh overhang on any product built solely on one US frontier API. Watch how it reprices the OpenAI/Anthropic IPO narratives. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: If you build on frontier APIs, design for portability — assume access can be revoked by policy, not just priced out. Keep a fallback model path and an abstraction layer.
- Action: Read
- Link: https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5-after-us-government-order/

### 2. Cognition raises $1B at a $26B valuation — Devin now writes 89% of its own code
- Category: Interesting Company / Startup
- Why it's interesting: A coding-agent startup more than doubled its valuation in eight months ($10.2B → $26B) while its own AI software engineer, Devin, now writes 89% of the code Cognition's engineers commit. Run-rate revenue is ~$492M, up ~10x in 2026.
- Why it matters: It's the clearest hard datapoint that autonomous coding agents have crossed from assistance to substitution inside at least one organisation — and that enterprises are paying for it, not just trialing it.
- Investment angle: Coding agents are the first agent category with proven willingness-to-pay and a path to public-market scale; the $26B mark becomes the comp that reprices rivals (and raises the bar for new entrants). (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: A live template for "AI eats its own workflow" — useful diligence question for any tool startup: what % of your own work does your agent already do?
- Action: Track
- Link: https://cognition.ai/blog/funding-growth-and-the-next-frontier-of-ai-coding-agents

### 3. Jensen Huang calls Marvell the next trillion-dollar company
- Category: Money / Market / NVIDIA / Compute
- Why it's interesting: A single comment from Nvidia's CEO sent Marvell up ~32% in a day — its biggest one-day gain ever — spotlighting custom silicon and interconnects as the next leg of the compute trade.
- Why it matters: It signals where Nvidia thinks value accrues beyond its own GPUs: ASICs, networking, optical and memory. The "halo" names are increasingly set by who Huang publicly anoints.
- Investment angle: Custom-silicon and interconnect/optical suppliers ride Nvidia's gravity as buildouts broaden past raw GPUs; treat CEO "anointing" events as momentum catalysts, not fundamentals. Qualitative read, no live prices. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: The compute bottleneck is migrating to packaging, memory and networking — relevant for anyone modelling where surrogate/inference workloads will actually run and at what cost.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/02/jensen-huang-nvidia-marvell-technology-trillion-dollar-ai.html

### 4. Nvidia and NAVER to build gigawatt-scale "sovereign AI factories" in Korea
- Category: Product / Model / Adoption (infrastructure)
- Why it's interesting: NAVER and Nvidia agreed to build gigawatt-scale AI factories on Nvidia's DSX platform (starting ~55MW, scaling toward 200MW by 2028) to train and serve Korea's HyperCLOVA X models — part of a national, government-backed sovereign-AI push.
- Why it matters: Whole nations are now buying full-stack Nvidia "factories" to avoid depending on US hyperscalers. Sovereign demand is becoming a primary, policy-backed vector for high-end compute, power and HBM.
- Investment angle: A durable new demand sink for GPUs, power and memory that also pre-allocates scarce HBM/CoWoS to nation-state programmes; watch which countries sign DSX-class deals next. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: Sovereign stacks create regional model anchors (HyperCLOVA X) and clearer compute access by geography — relevant to where deeptech infra can be built and funded.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10765602

### 5. Sakana AI's "The AI Scientist" passes peer review in Nature
- Category: Deeptech / Research Lens
- Why it's interesting: Sakana AI's system ran the entire research loop — idea, code, experiments, manuscript, and its own peer review — and a generated paper cleared the first round of peer review at a top ML conference workshop. Now published in Nature.
- Why it matters: Peer-reviewed publication moves "AI does AI research" from viral demo to the scientific record, and lowers the marginal cost of generating (variable-quality) research at scale.
- Investment angle: Value shifts from idea generation (commoditising) to the verification, evaluation and "taste" layer that filters machine output into real discoveries — that's the defensible part. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: For scientific-ML, the premium is on rigorous validation and autonomous-but-gated experimental pipelines; treat automated ideation as a throughput multiplier, not a replacement for verification.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist-nature/

## Money Map
Two rotations define the week. First, compute spend is broadening *past* the GPU: Huang anointing Marvell (+32% in a day) and Korea's sovereign Nvidia factories point capital toward custom silicon, interconnects, HBM and power — the picks-and-shovels layer. Second, capital is concentrating in agent startups that already bill real revenue, with Cognition's $26B mark setting the coding-agent comp. The new variable nobody was pricing is policy risk at the model layer: the US suspension of Fable 5 / Mythos 5 shows a frontier model can be switched off by directive, which bids up model-portability, evals and trusted-access tooling and adds an overhang to single-API startups. Names/themes to watch: ASIC and optical/interconnect suppliers, the OpenAI–Anthropic IPO setups, and the next sovereign-AI deals. Signal, not advice; built from public news, funding and pricing flow.
