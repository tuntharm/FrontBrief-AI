## Top 5

### 1. SpaceX agrees to buy Cursor-maker Anysphere for $60B all-stock
- Category: Must-Know — major M&A
- Why it's interesting: SpaceX signed a merger agreement on June 16, 2026 under which subsidiary X67 Inc. will absorb Anysphere — the company behind the breakout AI coding tool Cursor — in an all-stock deal implying a $60B valuation. Anysphere shares convert into SpaceX Class A stock based on SpaceX's seven-day volume-weighted average price; the deal is expected to close in Q3 2026 subject to regulatory approval. It exercises an option SpaceX secured in April (pay ~$10B for a partnership or buy outright for $60B later), and lands days after SpaceX's own record IPO, giving it freshly public stock to spend.
- Why it matters: It's one of the largest AI acquisitions ever and a statement that the AI-coding layer is consolidating into the mega-platforms — Cursor, founded in 2022, became one of the fastest-growing developer tools before being swept up. It also shows newly public AI-era giants using stock as M&A currency.
- Investment angle: Expect more roll-ups of high-growth app-layer tools by platform owners; watch the squeeze on standalone coding startups and competitive pressure on GitHub Copilot, OpenAI's Codex and Anthropic's Claude Code. (Signal, not advice; no live prices.)
- Tharm relevance: A live case study in how fast a category-defining dev tool can go from startup to strategic asset — and a reminder for founders that distribution-rich incumbents will pay up to own the workflow layer.
- Action: Read
- Link: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/spacex-spcx-cursor-acquisition-ipo.html

### 2. Mistral seeks ~€3B at a ~€20B valuation, pivots to "physics AI"
- Category: Interesting Company / Startup
- Why it's interesting: France's Mistral AI is in talks to raise roughly €3B ($3.5B) at about a €20B valuation — nearly double the €11.7B it carried in September — as it expands beyond chatbots into AI products aimed at industrial engineers ("physics AI"). Its ARR crossed ~€400M early in 2026 with a target of $1B by year-end, and the raise lands in a week that saw an estimated $15.8B pour into the "physics AI" theme across Mistral, Bezos's Prometheus ($12B) and PhysicsX ($300M).

- Investment angle: "Physics AI" / AI-for-engineering is the newly hot funding category; watch Mistral's enterprise and industrial traction and whether European sovereign-AI demand sustains the valuation. (Signal, not advice; no live prices.)

### 3. Nvidia and SK hynix lock in HBM4 memory for Vera Rubin through 2030
- Category: Money / Market / NVIDIA / Compute
- Why it's interesting: Nvidia and SK hynix signed a multi-year technology partnership to co-develop next-generation HBM4 high-bandwidth memory for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, with the deal also spanning Vera CPUs, RTX Spark PCs and Jetson Thor robotics systems. SK hynix is estimated to hold 60-70% of Nvidia's HBM4 allocation, with first deliveries planned for Q3 2026 — effectively locking memory supply through the end of the decade.
- Why it matters: As GPUs become more available, memory has become the toughest bottleneck in the AI supply chain. Securing HBM4 capacity is now as strategic as securing fab slots, and the resulting scarcity is pushing up build costs across the industry — including ordinary PC RAM.
- Investment angle: High-bandwidth memory is the new choke point; watch HBM4 share among SK hynix, Samsung and Micron, and how memory pricing flows into hyperscaler capex and AI unit economics. (Signal, not advice; no live prices.)
- Tharm relevance: When modelling where compute capacity and cost will sit, memory bandwidth and HBM availability — not just FLOPs — increasingly set the ceiling for large-model and surrogate-inference workloads.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/sk-hynix-ai-factory

### 4. Beijing forces Meta to unwind its $2B Manus acquisition
- Category: Strategy / Geopolitics — cross-border M&A
- Why it's interesting: China's National Development and Reform Commission ordered Meta to fully reverse its ~$2B purchase of AI-agent startup Manus — the first time Beijing has formally required an already-completed foreign acquisition to be undone — citing technology export-control and foreign-investment rules, on the basis that Manus's IP was developed on Chinese soil. Meta has now completed an operational split, told staff to stop using Manus tools and blocked Manus from its internal systems; the founders are reportedly seeking ~$1B to buy the company back at the same $2B mark.
- Why it matters: It's a landmark in AI techno-nationalism — a closed Western acquisition clawed back by a Chinese regulator — and a warning that cross-border AI deals carry reversal risk long after signing.
- Investment angle: Expect a higher discount on China-linked AI assets and more deal structures designed to insulate IP from export-control claw-back; watch how this chills US-China AI M&A. (Signal, not advice.)
- Tharm relevance: For a future founder/investor, this is the geopolitics of AI made concrete — where IP is "developed" can determine whether you can ever sell or transfer it across borders.
- Action: Read
- Link: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/meta-reportedly-begins-dismantling-2-billion-manus-deal-on-beijings-orders.html

### 5. PhysicsX raises $300M Series C to scale "physics AI" for engineering
- Category: Deeptech / Research Lens
- Why it's interesting: London-based PhysicsX raised an oversubscribed $300M Series C at roughly a $2.4B valuation, led by Temasek with participation from Nvidia, Siemens, Applied Materials, Atomico and General Catalyst. Its software replaces parts of traditional engineering simulation with ML surrogate models trained on simulation outputs plus real-world industrial data, predicting how systems behave under stress, heat, flow and vibration in seconds rather than hours — deployed across aerospace, semiconductors, automotive, energy and materials. The company says recognised revenue doubled year over year, booked revenue tripled, and headcount passed 300.
- Why it matters: It's the clearest signal yet that surrogate modelling is graduating from academic research into a venture-scale category, with strategics (Nvidia, Siemens, Applied Materials) backing AI that compresses the engineering design loop.
- Investment angle: Physics-AI / surrogate modelling is becoming a fundable vertical; watch enterprise adoption in aerospace, semiconductors and energy, and how specialists like PhysicsX defend against general labs (Mistral, Prometheus) entering the space. (Signal, not advice; no live prices.)
- Tharm relevance: This is your PhD thesis as a company — surrogate models replacing simulation. A direct template for the deeptech founder/investor lane: study their data strategy (sim + real industrial data), their go-to-market wedge, and how they price time-to-insight.
- Action: Track
- Link: https://www.physicsx.ai/newsroom/physicsx-announces-300m-series-c-to-accelerate-physics-ai-for-industrial-engineering
